# Related work

## LLMs in design ideation

Language models entered conceptual design as concept generators [@Zhu2022; @Ma2023] and were soon compared with people: their concepts are less diverse than crowdsourced ones [@Ma2025], their research ideas are judged more novel but slightly less feasible than experts' [@Si2024], and the product ideas they write have been benchmarked for creativity and diversity against human ones [@Terwiesch2026]. Studies of people working with AI report more creative individual output alongside less diverse collective output [@Doshi2024; @Anderson2024; @Ashkinaze2025], and more fixation when designers see AI-generated examples [@Wadinambiarachchi2024]. A second line builds pipelines around the model: task decomposition [@Wang2023], tools that enrich concepts with generated text and images [@Chen2024], multi-agent and agentic workflows [@Ding2023; @Liu2026; @Massoudi2026], and models that follow a design method, such as TRIZ [@Chen2024c; @Jiang2025; @Szczepanik2025], morphological analysis [@Chen2024b], analogical reasoning [@Kokate2026] or pattern-language design operations [@Tanaka2026]. Reviews of this field note how fast it moves and how much its findings depend on the model of the day [@Chiarello2024; @Chen2025]. Most of this work asks whether the output is good, or better than a baseline. Few studies hold the model, the method and the prompt fixed and vary only how the problem is presented, and fewer still report what each variant costs.

Personas are a common prompt device in this literature, from role-play that raises the creativity of LLM discussions [@Lu2024] to agents that act out the roles of a design team [@Liu2025]. They draw on long-standing ideas about thinking styles in innovation [@Bono1970; @Kelley2005]. Our follow-up treats the persona as a factor alongside the representation.

## Structured product representations

Design research has a rich set of structured descriptions of products: function–behaviour–structure [@Gero1990; @Gero2004], the functional basis [@Stone2000; @Hirtz2002], morphological analysis [@Zwicky1967; @Ritchey2006; @Ritchey2018], quality function deployment and the house of quality [@Hauser1988; @ed2024; @Chan2002], the design structure matrix [@Steward1981; @Browning2001; @Eppinger2012] and technology roadmaps [@Phaal2004]. The innovation matrix used here [@Tchirsky; @berengueres2014] belongs to this family: it links market needs to functions, components, technologies and fields in one layered graph. (It is distinct from the design-led innovation matrix of @Kyffin2009.) At a larger scale, technology semantic networks built from patents [@Sarica2020] support ideation by controlling the knowledge distance of what designers are shown [@Luo2021], and with human designers the distance of the stimuli shown affects the quality and novelty of what they produce [@Fu2013]. LLM work has begun to use such structures as input, for instance an FBS ontology to steer concept generation [@Chen2024a]. These representations were made for people, who read them selectively. How much of one to give a model, which reads everything it is given and pays for every token, is the question this paper takes up.

## Context, cost and efficiency in LLMs

More context is not free even when it fits in the window. Models use information in the middle of long inputs poorly [@Liu2024], their reasoning degrades as inputs grow long well before the limit [@Levy2024], and they struggle when relevant content must be inferred rather than matched [@Modarressi2025]. Comparisons of long-context models with retrieval find that retrieval reaches comparable results at a fraction of the cost [@Li2024]. A parallel literature makes cost a first-class outcome: cascades and routers that match strong models at lower cost [@Chen2024d; @Ong2024; @Ding2024], budget-aware evaluation of reasoning strategies [@Wang2024; @Han2025], the scaling of repeated calls [@Chen2024e], and agent benchmarks that report accuracy together with cost [@Kapoor2025; @Wang2025; @Gao2026]. This work concerns question answering, reasoning and agents, where an answer is right or wrong. Ideation has no single right answer, and its efficiency has rarely been measured.

## Measuring ideation

Ideation is usually assessed by quantity, quality, novelty and variety [@Shah2003], or by novelty and usefulness together [@Sarkar2011]. But the value of a brainstorm lies in its best ideas, since the rest are discarded: the quality of the best idea, not the average, is what idea-generation processes should be compared on [@Girotra2010], and extreme-value models describe how the best of many concepts improves with the number tested [@Dahan2001]. Mean scores, the usual summary in LLM ideation studies, can hide exactly this difference. We combine the two concerns, the tail and the cost, in one measure: top ideas per token.
