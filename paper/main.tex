\begin{abstract}

Instead of ephemeral human-vs-AI benchmarks, we evaluate representation efficiency:
standout ideas per total token spent. Using a 6-layer microwave oven knowledge graph (123
nodes, 246 edges), we test four prompt representations across 13 market needs and four
thinking-effort levels: full graph (A), market-need vertical slice (B), size-matched random
slice (R, controlling for prompt length), and no graph (C). Claude 3.5 Haiku generated
2,892 ideas (580 calls) under the SCAMPER Substitute operator, scored blind by Claude
3.5 Sonnet.

We measure yield: top-decile ideas (top 12\%, score $\ge 4.0/5.0$) per 100k total tokens
(input plus output/reasoning). The targeted market-need slice at medium effort achieves
peak yield (8.8 top ideas/100k tokens)---nearly triple the full graph and superior on
10/13 market needs. While the full graph produces more detailed average sets, it yields no
more top ideas and inflates token cost. An additional step of human expert analysis also
shows that, despite high scores, top ideas retrieve established state-of-the-art solutions rather
than unpatented inventions: LLM ideation operates as query-driven retrieval, where narrow,
structured context maximizes discovery throughput. Thinking longer raised linguistic quality 
metrics but not token efficiency. 


\end{abstract}
