=== USER ===
STUDY
Product: microwave oven
Target market need: M6: Simple operation with no learning curve
Every idea below was proposed to serve this need by substituting a part or a function of the
microwave with something cheaper or simpler.

WHAT A MOVE IS
A move is a distinct way of serving the need, defined by WHAT the idea replaces (a control, a
display, a setting step, a component or a user action) and BY WHAT (the principle that replaces it).
- Same move: an engineer would build both ideas on the same principle, even if names, numbers,
  materials or extra features differ.
- Different moves: the replacing principle differs. For example, a dial with food pictures,
  scanning a code on the food packaging, and a camera that recognises the food are three different
  moves, although all three replace the setting of time and power.
Aim for the level at which a product manager would brief different engineering teams. For about
250 ideas that is typically 20 to 50 moves; a move may cover a single idea.

CATEGORIES (one per move: the category of the move's main change)
- technology: a technical principle or component is the point of the move (a sensor, an actuator,
  a heating or power technology, a material)
- interaction: how the user tells the oven what to do, or how the oven tells the user (controls,
  display, voice, sound, app)
- behaviour: a decision, a step or the need to know something is removed (defaults, automatic
  start or stop, one-touch routines), using existing technology
- ecosystem: the move relies on something outside the appliance (food packaging, settings or
  recipes from a service, other devices in the home)
If a move both adds a technology and removes a step, choose technology when the new technology is
what would be patented, and behaviour when an existing technology is only re-used.

IDEAS (key. title, new elements, rationale)
O001. Tap-to-Add Heat Puck
   New elements: Single capacitive touch puck with LED ring (One raised puck: each tap adds 10 seconds, a ring of light fills as time builds, and a long press starts.); Sets duration with taps and no numbers (Replaces the rotary timer and its bell with one control that needs no reading.)
   Why: The mechanical timer knob becomes one puck that counts in taps and starts with a long press, so there are no numbers, modes or dials to learn.
O002. Press-Anywhere Door
   New elements: Full-width door release bar (The entire top edge of the door is a soft-touch bar; pressing anywhere along it releases the latch.); Flexible polymer cantilever snap latch (A molded cantilever that unlatches under any press along its length, replacing the lever and discrete button.); Releases the door with a press anywhere on the door edge (No target to find; a hurried or unsteady hand still opens the door on the first try.)
   Why: Removing the need to locate a specific small button means the door can be opened by feel alone, which suits users who have never used the appliance before.
O003. Door That Opens Itself
   New elements: Spring-cracked door latch (When the cycle ends, a spring eases the door open about 3 cm. There is no button and no lever to find.); Signals cycle completion by opening door (The open door replaces the bell as the 'done' signal and presents the food.)
   Why: The microwave opens itself when finished, so an elderly user never has to find a release button or remember to open it.
O004. Push-Anywhere Magnetic Door
   New elements: Magnetic over-centre catch with spring push-open (A neodymium magnet holds the door closed and a spring pops it ajar when pushed anywhere on the face.); Push-to-open magnetic catch (Replaces the release button and lever; the interlock microswitch still fires on latch travel.); Opens door on a push anywhere on the face (Removes the need to find and press a small button.)
   Why: A firm push anywhere on the door opens it, so the user never has to search for a button or learn where the release is.
O005. Pot-Lid Access
   New elements: Top-lift lid (A one-piece lid lifts straight up by a finger notch and stays open on its own, replacing the front door, hinges and push button.); Gas-spring counterweight (An off-the-shelf gas strut, as used in car tailgates, balances the lid so a light lift holds it open.); Quarter-wave choke seal (A metal choke channel around the rim blocks microwave leakage without a mesh window or latch.); Opens cavity by lifting, no button (The saucepan-lid gesture is universally understood and needs no latch or button.)
   Why: Lifting a lid like a saucepan needs no hinge to understand, no button to find, and no door swinging into the user's reach.
O006. Pull-Out Drawer Cooker
   New elements: Pull-out cooking drawer (The cavity slides out toward the user at waist height on a front-loading tray.); Slide rail with close-to-start switch (Ball-bearing rails; the final inward travel closes the interlock and starts the magnetron.); Starts cooking when drawer is closed (Pushing the drawer in is the start action, and pulling it out stops cooking.); Sliding seal must meet leakage limits (The moving choke seal must keep microwave leakage within limits over years of sliding.)
   Why: Pushing the drawer in is the one motion that both closes it and starts cooking, so nobody has to bend over a hot door or find a button.
O007. Tap-A-Plate NFC Cooking
   New elements: NFC-tagged plate and mug (Ordinary dishes carry a cheap passive NFC sticker that stores the recommended time and power for that meal.); Passive near-field reader in the cavity wall (A low-cost reader in the cavity wall reads the tag when the dish is placed inside and loads the cooking profile.); Reads cooking instructions from the dish (Transfers settings from the food container to the machine without any user input.); Requires tagged dishes and adds a per-dish cost (Works only with tagged containers; untagged dishes need a fallback.)
   Why: The dish tells the machine what to do, so the user just places their meal inside and presses start, with no settings to learn.
O008. Wave-to-Open Door
   New elements: Capacitive handle sensor (Electrode strip in the handle senses an approaching hand, so there is no button to find.); Capacitive proximity sensing (Electrode senses a hand within about 5 cm and signals the latch.); Motorised door latch (Solenoid latch releases only after the proximity signal and the interlock check pass.); Solenoid latch actuation (Electromagnetic latch with fail-safe, spring-return interlock.)
   Why: The door opens when the user's hand approaches the handle, so no button needs to be found, understood, or pressed.
O009. Touch-Anywhere Spring Door
   New elements: Full-width spring door bar (A flush bar across the whole top of the door; a light press anywhere on it springs the door open about 3 cm.); Opens door with a single press anywhere (Removes the need to find, aim at, or grip a small button and lever.); Spring catch with over-centre release (A spring-loaded catch that releases the interlock at under 5 N of force, usable by weak or arthritic hands.)
   Why: A door that opens from a press anywhere on its edge removes the hunt for a small button, so a first-time user opens it on instinct.

   Why: -
O011. Thermal Eye Replaces the Power Knob
   New elements: Sensing and closed-loop control (Using measured physical state to drive actuators automatically.); Infrared thermopile array over the cavity (Reads surface temperature of the food several times per second.); Closed-loop duty-cycle control (Firmware adjusts magnetron on/off ratio to reach a target temperature.); Auto-modulates heating to target temperature (Replaces the user's choice of power level with a single 'hot enough' goal.)
   Why: The five-step power knob and its cam are replaced by one goal the oven pursues on its own, so there is no power level to understand.
O012. Look-and-Go Auto Cook

   Why: A user with no settings to learn presses one button and the oven picks the power and time, so there is nothing to read, twist, or guess.
O013. Door-Open Auto-Start Cavity

   Why: Removes the start and release controls so the only action required is loading the plate and closing the door, which matches what an unfamiliar user already does.

   Why: The user taps the packet and the oven does the rest, so no dial, knob, or setting ever has to be understood.
O015. Voice-Free Gesture Hob: Wave to Start

   Why: A wave-to-start panel with no buttons to find or press removes the need to locate and understand controls, so the user just approaches and steps back.

O018. Say-It Microwave
   New elements: Microphone with keyword chip (A single MEMS microphone and a low-power keyword chip recognise a short fixed list such as 'warm milk', 'defrost', 'more' and 'stop'.); Accepts plain-speech cooking commands (The user says what they want and the appliance chooses the time and power.); On-device keyword spotting (A small local model recognises a fixed vocabulary offline, with no app, account or pairing.); Single STOP key fallback (One big stop key works when voice fails, so the cook can always be halted by touch.); Kitchen noise and accents can misrecognise commands (Extractor fans, clatter and accents reduce keyword accuracy.)
   Why: The user simply says what they want in ordinary words, so there is no dial, scale or power level to understand, and visually impaired users can operate it without looking at the panel.
O019. Tap-to-Cook Packaging
   New elements: NFC reader in the door's outer panel (Hold a food pack's tag against the front glass and the oven loads its time and power program.); Passive NFC tags printed on food packaging (A 13.56 MHz near-field tag stores the cook program and needs no battery.); Loads cook program from the food packaging (The packaging does the thinking, and the user only taps.); Metal cavity blocks in-cavity tag reading (Tags must be read through the front panel, not from inside the cavity.)
   Why: Users never set a time or power level because the food package carries the instructions and a single tap loads them.
O020. Stirred, Not Turned
   New elements: Rotating metal mode-stirrer vane (A small metal vane in the waveguide scatters microwaves so the field spreads evenly across a stationary plate.); Mode stirring by rotating reflector (A rotating conductive vane reshapes the standing-wave pattern to even out hot and cold spots.); Even heating without user repositioning (Food heats evenly wherever it sits, so the user never has to centre or turn the plate.)
   Why: Removing the turntable removes the one part a user has to load correctly, because the plate can go anywhere and still heat evenly.
